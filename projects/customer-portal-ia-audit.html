Customer Portal: Information Architecture & Quality Audit

#content-strategy #information-architecture #content-audit

Context

The customer-facing documentation portal had accumulated content across several generations of products. Customers searching for a legacy product name often couldn't find its current equivalent, while active and retired products were mixed within the same categories.

The result was a portal that was difficult to navigate and made it harder for customers to find the right documentation.

Information architecture assessment

I reviewed the portal's information architecture and documented the main issues with search, navigation, and the way current and legacy products were organised.

I then evaluated three options for restructuring the portal:

ApproachStructure
Functional groupingOrganise content by business function, with current and legacy products under each function
Version-firstOrganise by product line, with versions listed under each product.
Hybrid (recommended)Combine functional grouping with version and legacy status at the product level.

I recommended the hybrid structure because it provided a way to organise the portal by function while preserving product, version, and legacy relationships.

Post-implementation quality audit

I conducted a quality audit of the live portal to identify content and navigation issues.

The audit uncovered several issues, including:

  • Brand naming inconsistency — the product name appeared correctly in most places but incorrectly capitalized in others
  • Unreviewed content live on the customer-facing site — an internal, unproofread article was being served to customers where a validated version existed elsewhere
  • Duplicate knowledge base versions — two full versions of the same knowledge base existed simultaneously, with different update dates and nothing indicating which was current or authoritative.
  • Cross-category navigation — a link took users from product documentation to general support content without clearly indicating the change in context.
  • Stale content — a “News” panel hadn't been updated since an old release, despite newer ones having shipped.

For each issue, I documented the problem, its likely cause, proposed fix, and who should address it.

Result

The information architecture recommendations and quality-audit findings were documented and shared with the relevant teams, providing a clear set of priorities and actionable recommendations for improving the portal.
